# Challenge Overview

- Hackathon challenge: “AI for Product Decision Making” (BMW track)
- Core goal: objectify BMW product managers’ feature prioritization decisions
  - Currently every PM follows the same general process but applies personal preference
  - System should produce consistent, explainable outputs regardless of who runs it
- Scope: BMW Series 1, 5, and 7 across EU, China, and US markets
- Judging criteria confirmed from posted evaluation rubric:
  - Decision and scoring logic
  - Traceability and auditability (no black-box outputs)
  - Workflow and human decision-making integration
  - Solution quality, usability, and feasibility
  - Innovation, scalability, and business impact

# Customer Studies

- Separate sheets per market: China, EU, US (2025 only, no 2024)
- Structured survey scores across dimensions:
  - Exterior and interior styling, quality of materials, level of equipment
  - Safety, freedom of faults, value for money, reliability
  - Ease of instruments, all-round visibility, braking, sportiness, luggage, interior versatility
- Same car model (e.g. BMW 7 Series G70) appears twice in some sheets
  - Confirmed: different entries reflect market-specific variants, not a data error
  - Complexity noted: car configurations differ per market, making cross-market comparison non-trivial

# Feedback Hackathon

- ~4,000 rows of open-ended customer feedback per model
- Feedback pre-classified into four types: Likes, Defects, Wants, Difficult to Use
- Additional fields: model ID, source country (incl. Great Britain, Japan, Singapore), derivative/e-code (engine/drivetrain type, incl. ICE)
- VFC columns explained by Thomas (data colleague):
  - VFC Level 2: component category (e.g. “Steering Wheel”)
  - VFC Level 3: sub-component (e.g. “Steering Wheel Heating”)
  - Combined “VFC Level 2, 3” column: concatenation of both, redundant
  - Recommendation: extract unique values per column, build a taxonomy, then a knowledge graph
- Data quality issues noted:
  - Naming conventions inconsistent (freehand category labels, mixed structured/unstructured fields)
  - No standardized VFC classification applied uniformly

# Option List (PDFs)

- Public-facing documents; equivalents exist for competitor cars
- Confirmed useful for: understanding what features each car has, feeding into the comparison logic

# PM Guidance: Prioritization Logic

- Complaints and defects always outrank wish-list features
  - “If a lot of people complain, it’s an alarm bell. If it’s a nice-to-have, it’s lower priority.”
- Features benefiting a small user segment rank lower than broadly applicable ones
  - Example given: easy-to-clean carpets matter for dog owners but not universally
- Market-specific weighting is essential:
  - Sustainability: high priority in some EU markets, low elsewhere
  - Safety features (e.g. emergency alert): relevant in high-crime regions, not universal
  - Car series matters: Series 1 (entry-level) vs. Series 7 (high luxury) have different viable feature sets
- Adjustability built into the system is desirable:
  - PM should be able to tune market weights within the logic
  - Different PMs covering different regions should reach similar conclusions using the same framework

# Proposed Solution Architecture

- Input: PM enters a feature idea (e.g. “vacuum for pet hair removal”)
- System checks across:
  - BMW’s own sales and feedback data (structured + unstructured)
  - Competitor product specs and public reviews
  - Market-specific demand signals
- Output: objective yes/no recommendation with full reasoning
  - Includes regional nuance: “viable in EU, not recommended for China”
  - Reasoning must be traceable to specific data points, not LLM inference alone
- Must generalize to any feature input, not just predefined examples
- Explainability requirement: black-box LLM output alone is insufficient
  - Proposed approach: train a lightweight ML classifier (e.g. XGBoost or Explainable Boosting Machine) on LLM-labeled data
  - Advantage: fast training (under 2 minutes), no GPU needed, produces global and local explainability
  - LLM used for initial classification and labeling; ML model used for scalable, explainable inference
